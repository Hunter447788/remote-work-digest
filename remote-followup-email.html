How to Follow Up on a Remote Developer Application (With Examples)

Applications to remote roles can sit unread for weeks, or get answered in two days. A short, polite follow-up raises your chance of an answer without annoying anyone. The key is timing, length and making it easy to say yes or no.

When to follow up

Wait five to seven working days after applying. If the listing names a closing date, wait two working days after it. Follow up once, and if you hear nothing, follow up a second time after another week, then stop. Two silent follow-ups means the answer is no, and your time is better spent on the next application.

The three-line structure

Long follow-ups get skimmed. Three lines are enough:

  1. Reference: the role title and where you applied, so they can find your application fast.
  2. Value: one sentence on the strongest single thing you bring for this specific role, not a summary of everything.
  3. Easy out: a question they can answer in one line, such as asking whether the process is still open.

Example 1: one week after applying

"Hello [name], I applied for the Senior Full-Stack Developer role (remote, EU) last Tuesday and wanted to check in. I have shipped and maintained a TypeScript and Postgres service in production for three years, which matches the core of this role. Is the process still open, and is there anything else useful I can send?"

Example 2: after an interview, if they go quiet

"Hello [name], thank you again for the conversation about the platform role last week. You mentioned the next step was a small paid task. I wanted to confirm the timeline and say I am happy to start whenever it suits the team. Is the role still open?"

Mistakes that hurt you

Do not write "just checking in" with nothing else. Do not attach your full portfolio again or restate your whole CV. Do not follow up on weekends or ask why you were rejected in an angry tone. Every message to a team is a work sample of how you communicate asynchronously, and remote teams care about that more than office teams do.
