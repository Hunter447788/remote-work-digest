How to Answer the Timezone Overlap Question in a Remote Developer Interview

Many remote developer interviews include a question like this: "How many hours can you overlap with our core hours?" Interviewers ask because async work still needs a few shared hours for calls, reviews and decisions. A vague answer ("whenever needed") reads as evasive. A concrete answer with numbers builds trust immediately.

Step 1: Find the team's core hours

Look for a phrase in the job listing, for example "core hours 10:00 to 16:00 CET" or "standup at 9:30 am ET". If the listing gives a city, assume the team works roughly 09:00 to 18:00 in that city's timezone, Monday to Friday. If nothing is stated, ask the recruiter for the team's core hours before the interview.

Step 2: Convert to your own timezone

Write down the team's start and end times in your local time. Count the hours that fall inside your normal working day. That number is your overlap. Be honest with yourself: an overlap that requires you to work from 23:00 to 01:00 every day is not sustainable, and saying so later costs more than saying so now.

Step 3: Build your answer from three parts

A strong answer names the number, shows the math, and states your boundary:

  1. The number: "I can offer 4 hours of overlap with your core hours."
  2. The math: "Your 10:00 to 16:00 CET is 16:00 to 22:00 for me, and I normally work until 20:00, so we share 16:00 to 20:00."
  3. The boundary: "Outside that window I am async: I check messages once in the morning and answer by the next overlap."

Two example answers

Strong overlap: "I am in the same timezone as your core hours, so I can do the full 10:00 to 16:00 window. I prefer our daily sync early in that window and deep work after it."

Partial overlap: "My working day overlaps 3 hours with your core hours, from 15:00 to 18:00 your time. I can attend one daily call in that window. For anything urgent outside it, we can agree on a slow-response channel with a 24-hour answer time."

If your overlap is small

Do not hide it. State the number, then offer a mitigation: a weekly call at a time that costs you one early morning, clear written handoffs at the end of your day, and a public question log so nobody waits for you. Teams reject surprises, not timezones.
