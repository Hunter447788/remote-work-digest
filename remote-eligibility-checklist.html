Remote Job Eligibility Checklist

A free checklist for developers applying to remote roles. Check these six points before you spend time on an application. The same checklist ships inside the Remote Developer Application Kit as a 50-row worksheet.

The six checks, with what to write down

Run the checks in order and write one short answer per check. Six lines of notes decide whether the application is worth an hour.

  1. Country eligibility. Many remote roles are restricted to specific countries for tax or legal reasons. Write: the exact location sentence from the listing, or "not stated".
  2. Working hours. Check whether the role expects overlap with a specific time zone. "Remote" does not always mean "any time zone". Write: the overlap requirement and your answer in the employer's time zone.
  3. Contract type. Note whether the role is employment, contractor or agency, and whether that works for your situation. Write: the contract type, and "works" or "check my country's rules".
  4. Core skills. List the three to five core requirements. If you cannot point to real evidence for most of them, the application is likely a poor use of your time. Write: each requirement and the piece of your work that proves it.
  5. Application channel. Follow the employer's stated process exactly. Do not invent a faster route. Write: the channel and the exact next step it asks for.
  6. Next action with a date. Every application in progress should have one specific, dated next step. Undated intentions do not get done. Write: the action and the date you will do it.

Example: the six lines for one listing

A listing for a "Senior Backend Engineer, remote, EU time zone" produces notes like these:

  1. "Remote (EU only)": I am in the EU, eligible.
  2. "4 hours overlap with CET": I can offer 09:00-13:00 CET daily.
  3. Employment contract: works for me.
  4. Go, PostgreSQL, Kafka: I can point to two production services; Kafka needs a learning note.
  5. Apply via the form on the company site only.
  6. Fill the form and tailor the note on Kafka, 2026-10-15.

Six lines took five minutes and they decided the next hour.

Sample question to send

When a listing does not state a restriction clearly, ask early, politely and briefly:

Hello, thank you for posting this role. Could you confirm whether the position is open to candidates based in [your country], and whether any working-hours overlap is expected? I want to make sure I am eligible before investing your time in the process.

Replace the bracketed placeholder, keep it to one short message, and send it through the employer's official channel.
