How to evaluate a sales listing before applying

Sales postings reward a different kind of skepticism than engineering listings, because pay depends on variables the posting controls. Read each listing against these four checks before spending an application on it:

  1. Commission structure: does the posting state the split, the accelerators and the cap? "Uncapped commission" without a base or a published split is a cost to you, not a benefit. A base salary plus a defined variable is the structure you can actually budget against.
  2. Territory and quota realism: named territories like "East Region" or "Kansas City" mean your pipeline depends on a market the company chose. Ask whether the quota was met by the previous holder; a posting that cannot answer has usually priced the territory optimistically.
  3. Region and timezone requirements: many of this week's listings name a location (LATAM, Nagoya, Chicago, San Francisco) because sales coverage follows customer hours. If your timezone does not match the territory, the application is dead on arrival - check first, not after the first call.
  4. Role seniority and onboarding: "Early Career Sales Talent" and "Future Opportunities" listings are pipelines, not open roles. Treat them as low-cost networking, not applications with a decision attached.
