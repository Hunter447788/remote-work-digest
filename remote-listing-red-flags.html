5 Phrases in a Remote Job Listing That Signal Location Restrictions

You can save an hour of application work by reading the listing carefully before you apply. These five phrases appear in listings marked "remote" that are not fully open to every country. Each one comes with a concrete check you can run first.

1. "Remote (US only)" or a country list in parentheses

The listing names specific countries. If your country is not in the list, the employer cannot hire you there, usually because of payroll, tax or legal setup. Check: search the listing for your country name. If it is absent, ask before investing effort, or move on.

2. "±4 hours overlap with CET" (or any timezone requirement)

A timezone overlap requirement is a hard scheduling constraint, not a preference. Check: convert your working hours into the listing's timezone and count the overlap. If you cannot offer the stated number of hours, the role will not work as described. If you can, say so in exact numbers when you apply.

3. "Remote-friendly" or "hybrid" instead of "remote"

"Remote-friendly" often means the office is still the default. "Hybrid" means scheduled days on site. Check: if the listing does not say "remote" as the primary working mode, expect office presence. Look for phrases like "work from anywhere" only when they appear with the words "employment" or "contract".

4. "Contractor through an employer-of-record" or "EOR"

Some employers hire remote workers only as contractors via third-party companies. Whether that works for you depends on your country's rules on self-employment, taxes and social insurance. Check: your country's rules for foreign contract income before applying, so a later offer does not surprise you.

5. "Occasional travel to [city]" or "quarterly team on-sites"

Travel expectations are easy to miss and expensive to discover late. Check: how often, for how long, and who pays. International travel several times a year can cost more than you expect and may need visas you do not hold.

How to get a clear answer before applying

The fastest check is a two-line eligibility email: quote the exact phrase from the listing, state your country and your overlap hours in the employer's timezone, and ask whether that is eligible.
